# 📊 HR Analytics Dashboard & Workforce Insights

## 📌 Executive Summary
This project presents a comprehensive **HR Analytics Dashboard** built to track workforce dynamics, employee turnover, and talent acquisition trends. By analyzing historical and current employee data, the dashboard provides actionable insights into headcount stability, attrition drivers, manager-level turnover, and tenure-based retention patterns.

---

## 🖥️ Dashboard Architecture & Structure

The interactive dashboard is structured into three dedicated analytical views:

1. **Overview Page (Key Metrics & Trends):** Tracks macro-level KPIs including total headcount, new hires, terminations (voluntary vs. involuntary), and month-over-month headcount comparisons.
2. **Turnover Page (Attrition & Demographic Analysis):** Deep dives into the root causes of employee separations, turnover by manager, age distribution, experience breakdown, and tenure groups.
3. **Details Page (Granular Data Table):** Provides employee-level details with dynamic cross-filtering capabilities by Year, Department, Position, and Employee Name.

## 📈 Key Data Insights & Analytical Findings

### 1. Separation Trends & Seasonality
* **Peak Turnover:** A sharp spike in employee terminations occurred in **April 2018 (2018-04)**, where 3 separations were recorded in a single month—exceeding historical monthly averages.
* **Hiring Lag:** New hire activity remained flat (0 hires) across most months in 2018, leading to a downward trend in total headcount from `219` (Jan 2018) down to `214` (July 2018).

### 2. Turnover by Management & Tenure
* **Manager Distribution:** Terminations are distributed across specific managers (e.g., Amy Dunn, Elijiah Gray, Kissy Sullivan, Simon Roup, Webster Butler all account for 3 terminations each).
* **High-Risk Tenure Group:** The majority of voluntary separations occur within the **36–60 months (3–5 years)** tenure range (10 voluntary separations), indicating a critical retention threshold where mid-career employees tend to look for external opportunities.

### 3. Demographics & Experience Profile
* **Age Distribution:** The primary workforce segment belongs to the **35–50 age group** (151 employees), followed by **50+ age group** (64 employees). Younger workforce (<35 age group) represents a minor portion (6 employees).
* **Experience Breakdown:** The majority of the workforce possesses **36–60 months** (116 employees) and **60+ months** (89 employees) of experience, showing a highly mature workforce structure.
